Monte Carlo Block Bootstrap — Methodology & Results

Methodology Note

The bootstrap resampling procedure applied here blocks by trading date — on each of the 5,000 iterations, a sample of trading days (with replacement, same size as the original) is drawn, and the portfolio mean PnL per trade is computed from all trades on those days. This design preserves intra-day trade clustering and avoids inflating degrees of freedom.

The resulting distribution characterises the variability of the portfolio mean under plausible alternative realisations of the same market regime. A high P(mean < 0) — above the 95% threshold — indicates the portfolio would most likely remain loss-making even if the same strategy had been run on a different, randomly-drawn sequence of the same trading days.

Combination Filter — AND Logic: Passes All Three Simultaneously

Combination Filter Design

A trade is retained in the combination scenario if and only if it simultaneously satisfies all three conditions: (1) it falls outside any macro event blackout window (±30 min), (2) the prior-day VIX reading is below 20, and (3) the entry price is directionally consistent with the 6-hour rolling VWAP. FDAX slots are excluded from this analysis, as VWAP data is not available for that instrument.

The AND logic was specified prior to observing any combination results. This is the most conservative interpretation — any trade failing even one filter is excluded on the grounds that at least one conditioning hypothesis identifies it as unfavourable.

Step 4 Gate — Formal Criteria Assessment
Study Scope and Context

What Was Tested

Three independent conditioning filters were evaluated: a macro event blackout window (excluding trades within ±30 minutes of 10 categories of USD macroeconomic releases), a volatility regime gate (restricting to sessions where the prior-day VIX closing level was below 20), and a VWAP directional alignment filter (requiring trade direction to be consistent with the 6-hour rolling VWAP at entry time). All three were also evaluated in combination using AND logic.

Universe Studied

The 21 slots that survived the Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate correction during in-sample training: ES (Mon–Fri, multiple intraday windows), NQ (Mon, Thu, Fri, multiple windows), CL (Thu 20:00, Sun 18:00), and FDAX (Mon/Thu). The holdout period is July 2025 through July 2026, representing approximately 13 calendar months of out-of-sample performance never seen during model development.

Rigour of the Methodology

Each filter scenario was evaluated using a 5,000-resample Monte Carlo block bootstrap (blocking by trading date to preserve intraday clustering), a 13-month walk-forward monthly decomposition, and a per-slot Bonferroni-adjusted t-test. The combination scenario additionally required satisfying a three-criterion advancement gate before any further inference was drawn.

What Was Not Tested

These tests evaluated existing strategy trades against conditioning rules. Alternative signal architectures, revised entry logic, different instruments, or fundamentally different model structures were outside the scope of this study. The findings are specific to the current reversal strategy as implemented.

Filter-by-Filter Summary

Filter A — Macro Event Blackout

Removing 11.1% of trades (those falling within ±30 minutes of a macro release) did not improve portfolio performance. The bootstrap mean shifted from −$27.16 to −$29.16 per trade, with P(mean < 0) remaining at 1.0000. This is consistent with macro event timing being largely orthogonal to the performance patterns of the candidate slots, many of which operate in off-peak or overnight sessions.

Did not meet threshold criteria

Filter B — Volatility Regime (VIX < 20)

Restricting trading to low-volatility sessions (prior-day VIX below 20) reduced the trade count by 24.6%. Portfolio mean shifted from −$27.16 to −$29.45 per trade. The March 2026 month — where VIX exceeded 20 for most of the month — was nearly absent from this scenario (n = 780 vs. 6,715 unfiltered), yet performance did not improve in the remaining periods. P(mean < 0) = 1.0000.

Did not meet threshold criteria

Filter C — VWAP Directional Alignment

The most trade-restrictive individual filter, removing 48.7% of non-FDAX trades, produced the most modest improvement: −$25.14 vs. −$27.16 per trade. However, with P(mean < 0) = 0.9968 across 5,000 bootstrap resamples, this remains above the 95% confidence threshold. The wide P5–P95 interval (−$39.91 to −$10.88) reflects reduced sample size rather than a genuine shift toward profitability.

Did not meet threshold criteria

All Three Combined (AND Logic)

Applying all three filters simultaneously retained 18,619 trades (31.6% of the non-FDAX holdout). The observed mean was −$34.07 per trade. The combination satisfied one of three Step 4 advancement criteria (t-statistic change of +0.845, attributed to variance inflation from the smaller sample), but did not satisfy the slot improvement rate criterion (7/16 slots improved, threshold 8/16) or the significance criterion (0 newly significant slots).

Step 4 gate not satisfied — Step 5 not triggered
Consolidated Summary and Forward Perspective

Study Summary

This study applied three independent, pre-specified conditioning hypotheses to the 21-slot holdout portfolio: macro event avoidance, volatility regime selection, and VWAP-based directional alignment. Each was tested individually using a full Monte Carlo block bootstrap and monthly walk-forward decomposition. Their combination was subsequently tested using AND logic with a formal three-criterion gate.

None of the four tested scenarios produced a result consistent with a conditioning-based improvement in portfolio performance. The bootstrap P(mean < 0) ranged from 0.9968 to 1.0000 across all scenarios. Monthly decomposition confirmed that the pattern of results is consistent and distributed — not concentrated in any single month — ruling out a temporal anomaly as the driver.

The gross-versus-net PnL comparison completed earlier in this study confirmed that execution quality (slippage, commission) is not a material contributor to the observed performance. The three conditioning filters now confirm that the performance is not recoverable through trade timing (macro avoidance), regime selection (low-VIX sessions), or directional pre-screening (VWAP alignment).

These findings, taken together with the earlier in-sample validation work, point toward signal-level refinement as the most productive area for further investigation. The calendar-slot feature set — day-of-week, intraday bucket, and instrument — as currently constructed does not appear to encode durable directional predictive information of sufficient magnitude in the holdout period. Alternative feature engineering, model architectures, or strategy objectives (e.g. volatility targeting rather than directional reversal) would represent the natural next directions for this research programme.
